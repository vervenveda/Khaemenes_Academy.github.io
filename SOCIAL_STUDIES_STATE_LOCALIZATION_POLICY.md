# Khaemenes Academy · Social Studies State Localization Policy

## Permanent Academy rule

Every Khaemenes Academy Social Studies course is built as a **nationally portable disciplinary core with a required state/local overlay**.

This rule applies across the Academy's Social Studies progression, from Kindergarten through secondary study. The national core defines the disciplinary progression, inquiry/evidence expectations, essential competencies, assessment architecture, and Academy mastery policy. The state/local overlay adapts appropriate examples and evidence to the learner's jurisdiction.

## What should be localized when developmentally appropriate

Educators and course builders should localize:
- state and local geography, maps, landforms, waterways, resources, environments, regions, hazards, transportation, and settlement patterns;
- state/local history and chronology;
- specific Indigenous nations, homelands, governments, treaties, historical experiences, sovereignty, and present-day communities;
- state constitutions, state/local government, elections, courts, public services, civic institutions, and public decision processes;
- state/local African American history, migration, labor, industry, economy, public service, civil-rights history, cultural institutions, and historical figures;
- archives, museums, libraries, newspapers, photographs, oral histories, public records, historic sites, official data, and other state/local source sets;
- age-appropriate economic and financial-literacy examples using state/local industries, employment, taxes, public services, trade, or resource choices;
- research capstones and informed-action cases when safe, nonpartisan, privacy-preserving, and evidence-ready.

## National core invariants

Localization may **not**:
- lower the Academy's 80% mastery threshold;
- eliminate required inquiry, geography, history, civics, economics, culture, source/media literacy, or informed-action competencies;
- replace evidence-based Social Studies with state trivia or tourism facts;
- erase Indigenous sovereignty, historical presence, or present-day continuity;
- present one cultural group, region, or community as internally uniform;
- require sensitive personal disclosure;
- make partisan advocacy or support/opposition to a candidate or party a course requirement;
- reduce cumulative assessment, correction, or fresh-form reassessment standards.

## Course architecture

Each grade should maintain:
1. a canonical national course;
2. explicit machine-readable metadata showing that state localization is required;
3. a grade-specific localization guide naming the weeks/units where a state/local overlay belongs;
4. a standards crosswalk showing how state additions fit the canonical course;
5. state/local source guidance and privacy safeguards where appropriate.

Whenever possible, create **state packs or overlays** rather than separate state-specific forks of the whole course. This preserves one canonical Academy course while allowing Florida, California, New York, Texas, and other jurisdictions to add their required history, geography, government, economics, and civic examples.

## Mastery rule

State localization changes context, examples, and evidence—not the bar for mastery. Khaemenes Academy's 80% mastery policy remains the governing progression standard.

## Public-site safety

No state/local adaptation may expose a learner's exact home address, immigration/citizenship status, political affiliation, religion, family finances, private family history, or other sensitive personal information. Public, fictional, teacher-curated, or officially published examples are acceptable substitutes.
